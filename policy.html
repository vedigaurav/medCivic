Connecting Policy to People

MedCivic engages in research and dialogue on DNS policy issues that impact public health, consumer protection and trust online. We support a multistakeholder approach to Internet governance and aim to give a youth and community lens to these discussions.

We believe that the rules shaping the Internet should reflect the needs of the communities who rely on it most. By participating in global policy forums and contributing youth-led perspectives, we work to ensure that health equity and digital safety remain central to how the Internet is governed.

Our Approach

We center youth and community voices in conversations that are often dominated by technical and corporate stakeholders. Our work bridges grassroots health advocacy and global Internet policy.

ICANN & DNS Policy

MedCivic participates in the multistakeholder processes that shape the Domain Name System and the broader governance of the global Internet.

ICANN Participation

We engage with ICANN's At-Large Advisory Committee (ALAC) and related bodies to represent the public interest, including underrepresented communities, in decisions about domain names and Internet identifiers.

DNS & Public Health

We research how DNS infrastructure and domain policies intersect with the spread of health misinformation, access to telehealth services, and the availability of trustworthy health resources online.

Consumer Protection

We advocate for stronger consumer protections in domain registration processes, including transparent WHOIS policies, anti-abuse mechanisms, and safeguards against deceptive health-related domains.

Multistakeholder Model: MedCivic supports the multistakeholder model of Internet governance, which ensures that decisions about the Internet are made with input from governments, the private sector, civil society, and the technical community — not any single entity.

Key Policy Areas

MedCivic monitors and engages on the following Internet governance issues that have direct or indirect consequences for community health and wellbeing.

Health Misinformation & Domain Abuse

Combating the registration and use of deceptive domains that spread false health information or impersonate legitimate medical providers.

Access to Telehealth Services

Ensuring that DNS infrastructure and connectivity policies support equitable access to telehealth platforms, especially for underserved and rural communities.

Privacy & WHOIS Reform

Balancing transparency in domain registration with the privacy rights of individuals and civil society organizations operating in sensitive health contexts.

New gTLD Program & Health Namespaces

Advocating for responsible stewardship of health-related top-level domains (e.g., .health, .pharmacy) to protect consumers and ensure genuine public benefit.

Youth Digital Rights & Safety

Promoting Internet governance policies that protect young people online, including safeguards against predatory health marketing, cyberbullying, and exploitation.

Multilingualism & Health Equity

Supporting internationalized domain names (IDNs) and multilingual Internet resources to ensure that health information is accessible to communities in their own languages.
